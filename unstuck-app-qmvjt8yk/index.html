This isn't a habit app asking for a streak you can't keep. It's four small tools pulled straight from the book — built for the days willpower doesn't show up.

There's no login, no setup, and no correct order. Tap whichever one matches what's happening right now:

  • Can't get started? Open the Ignition Timer.
  • Day feels too big? Open Three-Thing Day.
  • Want to see how you're doing? Open Your Progress.
  • Everything fell apart? Open the 3-Question Reset.

Every screen has a small "i" button next to its title with a fuller explanation of why it works and how to use it — tap it any time you want a reminder.

One honest note: this is an organizational tool, not therapy or a medical device. It doesn't diagnose or treat anything, and it isn't a substitute for care from a licensed therapist or doctor.

Ignition Timer

You're not promising to finish. You're promising ten minutes.

Why it helps

Starting is the hard part for a stalled-out brain — not finishing. Ten minutes is small enough that it's almost harder to say no to it than to just do it.

How to use it
1.Pick 5, 10, or 20 minutes — whichever feels smallest right now.
2.Tap Start and do the one thing in front of you, even badly.
3.When it rings, you're free. Keep going if momentum showed up — stop if it didn't. Both count as a win.
4.You can put the phone down or switch apps — the clock keeps running, and it chimes when the time is up. If your browser closes it in the background, the app still knows: you'll see the time was up when you come back.

Three-Thing Day

Exactly three. There's no fourth line here — on purpose.

Why it helps

A ten-item list is a reason to do nothing at all. Three open slots make overload impossible — there's simply no room to pile on.

How to use it
1.Write only three things — resist adding a fourth, even a small one.
2.Make at least one of them almost embarrassingly small.
3.Check them off as you go. All three done means the day is finished — no matter what's still sitting on the shelf.
Make one of them small enough that skipping it would feel almost silly.

Your Progress

Tap a day to mark it. A missed day only costs that one day.

Why it helps

Most streak trackers punish a missed day by wiping out every day before it. That's not how this works — a gap doesn't erase what came before, and it doesn't disqualify what comes next.

How to use it
1.Tap any past or present day when you showed up, in whatever small way that meant.
2.Missed one? Leave it blank and move on — nothing else changes.
3.The number at the top counts days that counted, not days in a row. That's the whole point.

3-Question Reset

Answer at your own pace. This is already the way back.

Why it helps

When things fall apart, the hardest part is deciding where to re-enter. This narrows it down to one tiny action, one honest capacity check, and one thing you have permission to let go of.

How to use it
1.Answer each question in a sentence or less — there's no wrong answer here.
2.Pick the capacity level that's true right now, not the one you wish were true.
3.Name one thing that can wait. Then take the small step you named — that's the reset.
